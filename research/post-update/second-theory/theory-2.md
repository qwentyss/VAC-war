# Post-Update Theory 2 — Context-Aware ESP

## Status

**Status:** Proposed — unverified

**Research phase:** Post-update

**Category:** Hypothesis about context-aware visual assistance

## 1. Hypothesis

This theory considers a hypothetical ESP system designed to limit the information it displays according to what a player could reasonably perceive during normal gameplay.

The proposed concept is a context-aware ESP that accounts for three conditions:

- **Smoke:** Enemies obscured by smoke would not be visually revealed by the overlay.
- **Flash effects:** Enemies would not be revealed while the player's vision is impaired by a flash effect.
- **Sound awareness:** Enemies would not be revealed solely through the overlay when their presence has not been established through naturally available in-game information, such as audible footsteps or other sounds.

The underlying idea is to make the displayed information more consistent with the player's ordinary in-game perception.

## 2. Research Question

Does limiting an overlay's displayed information according to in-game visibility, visual impairment, and available sensory information change its observable behavior compared with an unrestricted overlay?

A separate question is whether any such changes have a measurable relationship with anti-cheat enforcement. This cannot be assumed from the overlay's apparent behavior alone.

## 3. Assumptions

This hypothesis depends on several unverified assumptions:

- Relevant visibility and visual-effect states can be identified accurately.
- Information about nearby opponents can be classified consistently.
- The distinction between naturally available information and hidden information can be defined objectively.
- The proposed behavior can be evaluated independently of assumptions about VAC's internal implementation.

None of these assumptions has been established by this document.

## 4. Limitations

- A more restricted overlay is not necessarily undetectable.
- Visual similarity to ordinary gameplay does not establish that software is compliant with game rules.
- An absence of an observed ban does not prove that anti-cheat systems failed to detect the software.
- A ban does not independently identify which software behavior or detection mechanism caused it.
- The hypothesis may be technically incomplete or based on inaccurate assumptions about available game information.

## 5. Evaluation Criteria

Any assessment should distinguish between:

1. Whether the proposed behavior is implemented as described.
2. Whether the displayed information matches the stated perception constraints.
3. Whether the available evidence supports or contradicts the hypothesis.
4. Whether any conclusions about anti-cheat behavior can actually be established from the evidence.

These are separate questions and should not be treated as interchangeable.

## 6. Current Conclusion

Theory 2 is a conceptual hypothesis only. No conclusion has been established about whether context-aware ESP changes the likelihood of anti-cheat enforcement.

The theory remains **unverified** until reliable evidence supports or contradicts its specific claims.

## 7. Disclaimer

This document records an independent research hypothesis. It does not describe a verified VAC detection mechanism, establish that the proposed concept is undetectable, or constitute an endorsement of cheating or violating game rules.
